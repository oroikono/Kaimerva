# Theme method

Use this reference when selecting a theme, evaluating an existing world or
translating a theme into an implementable design. These are examples to adapt,
not presets every site must include.

## A useful brief

Capture the desired impression in the person's words, who visits, and what those
visitors should find first. Choose a small number of meaningful destinations
from the real content: research, making, writing, performance, community or
other categories. Keep the destination labels intelligible without the metaphor.

For each destination, specify:

| Decision | Useful detail |
| --- | --- |
| Spatial object | Why this silhouette represents the content |
| Material and light | Surface roughness, scale, contact and contrast |
| Idle motion | One quiet behavior that belongs to the object |
| Selection response | What changes and how the reading panel opens |
| Navigation | Direct control, optional travel and fallback |

Sketch the overview and selected composition together. Reserve a readable
identity area, destination labels, recent activity and a clear way to reach
contact or a CV. Keep the person's face natural if they provide an authorized
portrait; the model need not depict an invented avatar.

## Learn mechanisms from references

Inspect the rendered page or relevant video frames. Note observation time and
source. Describe mechanisms: layered silhouettes, contact shadows, restrained
materials, a continuous environment, camera movement, readable content placement,
or a response that makes the visitor feel present. Distinguish observed behavior
from an inference about its implementation.

If a reference cannot render, say so. A source repository can establish controls
or architecture, but does not establish how its current live site looks.
Combine useful general mechanisms with the user's metaphor. Do not reproduce a
reference's distinctive models, composition, typography and choreography as one
package. Downloaded assets and copied code require their own reuse basis.

## Example theme matrices

Each row proposes a world with professional content. No accomplishment, image
rights or implemented feature is implied by these proposals.

| Theme | Research / evidence | Projects / making | Journal / perspective | Traveler and materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coast | Signal lantern beside a breakwater | Workshop with a small articulated crane | Trail journal on an offshore sailing craft | Simple vessel; limestone, timber, salt-worn metal, ivory canvas |
| Orbit | Optical instrument examining a suspended specimen | Modular construction gantry on a habitat | Survey capsule beside a star chart | Small capsule on authored transfer arcs; ceramic, brushed metal, softly lit glass |
| Forest | Glasshouse with labeled specimens | Joiner's pavilion and mechanical arm | Field shelter with a hanging journal | Small cart on marked paths; wood, moss, stone, translucent panes |

Adapt movement to the object: a beacon sweeps, an instrument tracks, a crane
articulates, a canopy stirs. Keep buildings and reading text stable. Animate one
component with purpose rather than rotating every object. Hover and focus can
strengthen a response; selection should noticeably change the composition.

For a new theme, change the geometry, scale, spatial arrangement, lighting and
motion grammar. Decide which interactions survive unchanged because they serve
the portfolio, and which would conflict with the new metaphor.

## Two exemplar briefs

**Orbit, materials researcher.** Intended impression: calm curiosity and careful
construction. Evidence, Builds and Notes are three distinct instruments around a
stationary habitat. Warm ceramic surfaces contrast with a dark blue environment.
The evidence instrument turns toward a selected specimen; Builds articulates one
gantry joint. Selecting either immediately shows an HTML title, summary and
source links. A capsule may trace a short transfer while the visitor reads.
On phones the environment sits above the panel; the same controls work without
WebGL. This is a design proposal, not a scientific visualization of results.

**Forest, independent developer and writer.** Intended impression: patient
exploration and craft. Work lives in a joiner's pavilion, Experiments in a
glasshouse, and Writing in a field shelter. The traveler follows authored paths;
no global keyboard trap is introduced. Only foliage and one workshop mechanism
move at idle. Latest updates and the normal work index remain easy to reach.
The content is supplied by the person; demo examples must be labeled as demos.

## Static and motion review

A static screenshot should already have convincing depth: overlapping objects,
contact, differentiated materials and a composed camera. Motion can reinforce
that composition, not rescue it. Compare at least the overview, a selected
destination and a phone layout. If all objects look interchangeable or the
metaphor hides the work, revise the brief before adding effects.

Avoid entry gates, forced scrolling, meaningless progress scores and invented
research plots. Session counters can describe actual arrivals or selected
entries; they should not imply reading, learning or professional achievement.
